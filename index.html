Spend

TikTok is burning cash below breakeven

September final: ROAS 0.93 vs. Meta's 1.20, on 1.3× Meta's spend, for a worse cost per sale (425 vs. 301 SAR). Last week it was 0.63 vs. 1.16, and October so far reads 0.73 vs. 1.28 (500 vs. 284 SAR per sale) while TikTok takes 52% of the spend. Still the clearest lever to improve CAC without adding budget.

Data quality

Two churn numbers, same dashboard

Overview's rolling 30-day churn is 28.5%, up from 28.3% on Oct 10 and 23.9% on Sep 27; the payment-failed part keeps rising (10.0% on Oct 3 → 13.0% now) while chose-to-leave eased to 15.5%. Retention's recommended "Proper SaaS" card only shows the current month, now Oct 1–18 (17%), so September's figure on that method isn't readable as a number. Neither should anchor a KR target until Maher confirms which one is canonical.

Quick win

A named, buyable fix for involuntary churn

8.6% of people with failed payments are on cards that "don't support purchase" (mostly Saudi cards on international rails). Retries only recover 20.0% of these, and they cause 22.2% of first-charge failures for brand-new customers. Enabling Mada is a bounded, concrete fix sitting directly in the data.

Revenue ceiling

Members leave before the upsell even starts

3.28-month average lifetime against a 12-month program, 27% completion on average. Half of starters are gone by their 3rd payment. Almost all Mind Power / Ezdhar revenue is currently unrealized.

Pipeline health

All data feeds are syncing

As of Oct 18 all 8 feeds in Settings → Sync Status ran successfully (GHL Contacts, Stripe, Meta, TikTok, GHL chat and orders, and the rollups). The status reads "Degraded" because two data checks flagged unusual days, including a spike of 19 failed payments on Oct 17. That's a business signal, not a sync failure. GHL Funnels, stale since ~Aug 25, is no longer listed. It isn't clear yet whether it was fixed, replaced by the new "GHL orders" feed, or retired.

Worth watching, not concluding

Week-1 completion doesn't predict staying, so far

At day 30, retention is 96% vs. 94% regardless of week-1 completion, and the day-60 gap from early reads has closed (60% vs. 59%, now on 96 and 114 members). Onboarding activation alone doesn't look like the churn lever; the drop-off at the 2nd and 3rd payment and failed cards look like bigger ones.
